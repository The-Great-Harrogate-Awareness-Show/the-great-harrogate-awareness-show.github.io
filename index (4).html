The founding year

A day of learning, inspiration and connection

This is not a conference and it is not a fundraiser with a raffle. It is a day where Harrogate's charities, support organisations, clubs and businesses stand alongside people who have lived through the hardest things a person can live through, and both are genuinely reachable, in one place, for the price of a cinema ticket.

On stage

Real stories, told properly

Suicide prevention, homelessness, disability, neurodiversity, business, sport. Told by people who lived them, with time for questions afterwards, not a talk and a rush to the next thing.

In the room

The help is in the room

Behind every story sits the local organisation already doing that work. You hear what happened, then you can walk ten feet and talk to the people who can help.

All day

Built for all ages

Children, teenagers, parents, grandparents. Sport, music, activities and a finale designed so three generations end the day on the same dance floor.

Where it comes from

It starts with a field near Harrogate

Steve Tappin's father began with little more than a field, a few small aircraft, and fewer than a hundred people paying about a fiver to come and watch. He kept growing it. The field got bigger. The aircraft got bigger. Helicopters arrived. Then the Red Arrows. Eventually it became the Yorkshire Air Show, up to 20,000 people a day.

In memory of Dave Tappin
He loved aircraft. But his real gift was bringing people together around an audacious idea, and making possible something others thought impossible.

The Show carries that spirit forward, in memory of David and Sylvia Tappin, and for everyone in this town who has been left behind by something. It begins small again, on purpose. A founding year. One field. One day.

Follow the Show
Running order

How the day runs

Come for an hour or stay for ten. Your ticket covers all of it, including the evening.

From 12 noon · Open

The Show begins

Doors, music across the site, and the opening session. Harrogate’s extraordinary town stories, hosted by John Reynolds. The founder tells the story of how this came to be.

Afternoon · Experience

Eight areas, running at once

Move between them freely. Every talk has time for questions afterwards, because the conversation matters more than the speech. Sport, children’s activities and the health and wellness quarter run throughout.

Late afternoon · Come together

Everyone in one place

Stories from the day are shared, partners are thanked, and young people and older generations come together. The music starts to shift.

8pm – 10pm · Celebrate

Dance the night away

The Show moves from awareness into celebration, and the day ends the way it should, with the whole town on the same floor.

8pm – 10pm · Celebrate
The Harrogate 80s Disco

Familiar songs, big choruses, and three generations on the same floor. Wear white if you like, in memory of David Tappin and his famous white disco trousers. Included in every ticket.

Where the money goes

Split three ways, in the open

No funds or proceeds are taken by the founder or the core contributors. The speakers are giving their time. After the costs of putting the day on, the net proceeds are split three ways.

The specific causes and communities represented at the Show.

If you're struggling right now

Parts of this day deal openly with suicide, mental health, homelessness and loss. The Show exists to connect people with help. It does not replace professional help, and it is not a crisis service.

If you need support today, please contact your GP, call NHS 111, or in an emergency call 999. Samaritans are available free, day or night, on 116 123.

There will be a quiet welfare space, trained staff and qualified safeguarding leads on site all day. Sessions covering difficult subjects will be clearly marked in the programme.
